About Me

I am a Mechanical Engineer and PhD student at the University of Houston with research interests at the intersection of mechanical systems, machine learning, and acoustics. My work focuses on understanding mechanical systems through data-driven methods, particularly vibration and acoustic signal analysis.

My current research explores machine learning techniques for fault detection, percussion sound analysis, and acoustic learning systems. I work with signal processing techniques such as spectral analysis, MFCC-based features, and time-frequency analysis to interpret mechanical and acoustic phenomena.

My broader interests include optimization of dynamic systems, automotive diagnostics, mechanical system monitoring, and intelligent sensing for engineering applications. I enjoy bridging traditional mechanical engineering with modern AI-driven approaches.

Beyond my professional work, I'm passionate about sharing knowledge through video tutorials and exploring creative storytelling through short films.

Research Projects

Non-Destructive Bolted-Flange Looseness Detection from Percussion-Induced Acoustic Signals

Machine Learning Competition | University of Houston

View Poster

  • Developed a non-destructive machine learning framework for detecting bolted-flange looseness using percussion-induced acoustic signals.
  • Classified flange conditions at three bolt preload levels: 0 ft-lb, 25 ft-lb, and 50 ft-lb.
  • Extracted spectral, MFCC, and physics-guided features from smartphone-recorded percussion signals.
  • Applied within-flange-area normalization (WFAN) and PCA-based feature transformation to improve cross-flange generalization.
  • Achieved 97–99% accuracy on completely unseen flanges, demonstrating strong robustness for practical looseness detection.

Dynamic Modelling and Response of a Pelton Bucket
